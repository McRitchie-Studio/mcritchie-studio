This card is about **how content gets made** — where ideas come from, how many
get shipped, what each one costs, and what you stop doing. What to *say* lives on
[`audience-and-avatar.md`](audience-and-avatar.md); why volume works lives on
[`volume-and-repetition.md`](volume-and-repetition.md).

## The claim

**He is not making most of his content. He is capturing it.**

> "I'm usually not making content. We're just capturing it."
> `XsWSvz-aewA`

The arithmetic against the alternative is the argument `XsWSvz-aewA`: if you have
to sit down and actively record every single thing, and you want 20 or 50 pieces a
day, you will do nothing but stand in front of a camera and it will be miserable.
So the selection rule is: **do the thing you like doing, and capture it.** For
him, that is Q&As with business owners — an activity he has had thousands of reps
at and which fills him up rather than draining him. At events, if his calendar is
blank, he goes downstairs and takes questions.

The whole card follows from that inversion. Your operating calendar is the
content supply. The content calendar is downstream.

## Where the ideas come from

### The strongest claim in the corpus: customer questions *are* the calendar

He asked a dietitian doing just under $1M a year off 5,800 Instagram followers
where she gets content ideas. The answer:

> "They all have questions and so I just make my posts answering their questions.
> That's it."
> `0S5xsICW8qg`

She has, in his words, **endless** sources of content because she does not invent
topics. The ideation step is deleted, and every post is indexed to real demand
rather than to what the creator finds interesting. Her account is 100% insurance
billing, zero nutrition; daily cadence; one soft CTA roughly every other post.

**Condition:** it requires an audience already asking you things — inbound
questions, sales calls, support tickets, DMs. If nobody is asking yet, the
meeting-mining play below is the substitute `0S5xsICW8qg`.

The same wheel, stated as a mechanism `reisEL_D7xc`: make content → people ask
questions about it → make content about those questions → they ask questions about
those. The second feed is internal — catalogue the friction points customers hit
implementing what you sell, and make a piece on making each one easier.

> "as long as people have problems, you're always going to have problems to solve"

### The four other sources, in order of how much proof they carry

| Source | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Mine your meetings** `XsWSvz-aewA` | Put a transcription plug-in on every virtual meeting, then prompt: *"Hey AI, tell me the interesting moments from these meetings"* and talk about those. Beats ideation because the output carries real examples rather than invented stories. He offers it explicitly as the **lesser** of two options |
| **Engineer proof into the delivery motion** `XsWSvz-aewA` | The better option — see *proof engineering* below |
| **Fresh off a real conversation** `eVY4Zo-hpqU` | Every video he makes is either documenting a thought process *for himself*, or made straight off a conversation with a client, employee or portfolio owner, anonymised. Two rules fall out: **do it when it's fresh, and do it when you feel emotions** |
| **The master content sheet** `MD5-HByRxoA` | One spreadsheet: your best-performing content of all time, plus the FAQs from sales calls. The sales use is a rep sending the matching piece between call one and call two; the marketing use is that the same list tells you what to make next |

### The hard filter on every idea `eVY4Zo-hpqU` ⚠ contrarian

> "If the source of your content is other people's content, don't make it."

What makes content interesting is the anecdotes you personally lived. The failure
mode he names: read an article on passive income, produce a worse, diluted version
with less authority. Or repeat research from a named expert — where the audience
would rather just go to that expert.

Two supporting moves for anyone with a thin record:

- **Scope the claim to what you observed.** *"I only have seven clients, but this
  has worked really well for the guys that I have. I hope it works well for you."*
  Nobody can question you inside that boundary; the scope **is** the safety
  mechanism `eVY4Zo-hpqU`.
- **Don't make declarative statements.** Say *this is my two cents, this is how I
  see the world and what I have experienced* rather than *this is the way it is*.
  A declarative claim is falsifiable by anyone with a different business;
  a report of your own experience is not `eVY4Zo-hpqU` `WrCt0R3FBFs`.

And if you have nothing to post, the problem is your **input** `7ITff1fIbSc`: *do
more cool so you have cool to talk about.* His own arc is the same claim as
history — the first 150 podcast episodes are just how to run a gym better, then
marketing, then sales, then M&A, tracking what he was actually doing at the time.

## Who the content is for — and the mistake he made

**Your team is not your buyer.** `JOfsujnXxCg` ⚠ contrarian

> "your team might not be the type of people you're trying to make content for"

He had been making videos **his team asked for** instead of videos for his avatar,
because team suggestions drove the topic list. He named the fix as one of six
simultaneous shifts — *"for us to for you"* — and the bundle measured:

| Metric | Change |
|---|---|
| RPM (revenue per thousand viewers) | **+68%** |
| Comments per view | +25% |
| Long-form views (channel-wide) | +29.56% |
| Subscriber conversion | +24.6% |
| Weekly opt-ins | +26% |
| Book sales | **doubled** |

On **fewer views per video**. He is careful that these are the bundle's numbers,
not any single shift's, and that he was "kind of lucky this worked out the first
time" — normally four of six stats would be bad.

**The comments will mislead you** `Jmkq5RLjm0U` ⚠ contrarian. A friend doing $10M a
year told him offhandedly he had stopped watching. The comments had loved the
wider styles; the people he could actually help had left. His replacement signal
is **in-real-life**: ask owners who fly out in person what their favourite format
is, and watch for texts from owners he respects `dMZ-n2KSlxE`. He also concedes
business owners *had* been telling him in the comments what they wanted and he
was not listening `JOfsujnXxCg` — so the rule is read between the lines of the
comments, not ignore them.

**And beware the vendor's metric** `MD5-HByRxoA` ⚠ contrarian:

> "Just because it's easy to quantify doesn't mean it's important."

Specialist vendors optimise clicks, views, impressions and subscribers because
those are easy to measure and because most clients know nothing else to ask for.
If only one specific kind of thing hits and it is not core to who you are, resist
it — or you build an audience doing work you do not want to do.

## Cadence — the five phases

`MD5-HByRxoA`

| Phase | What it is |
|---|---|
| 1 | Post something at all |
| 2 | A cadence on **one** platform you already use (his own: one day a week reserved for marketing work) |
| 3 | A reliable cadence across **all** platforms — a large jump in volume |
| 4 | **Max out** what each platform will take: short-form (X, TikTok, FB reels) absorbs 5–10/day; newsfeed platforms fatigue at 1–2 |
| 5 | Deliberately **create and capture** — e.g. run a call-in show and repurpose it everywhere |

At the time of recording he said he was barely scratching phase five and maxed out
on about half the platforms.

**The objective changes at phase three, and this is the part people skip.**
Phases 1–2 are **lead nurture, not lead generation** `MD5-HByRxoA` ⚠ contrarian.
If your bills are paid by outbound, affiliates, paid ads or referrals, do not take
your eye off those. Content's job there is that a prospect who arrived another way
checks your profile and concludes you are legitimate.

> "The purpose or the objective of having the organic content is lead nurture, not
> lead generation."

The only litmus test at that phase: **you posted something recently that is not
garbage.** He is explicit that this is a correction of his own older position —
six years earlier he told Gym Launch clients to stop making content entirely, and
says he was partially right (as lead gen for an uninvested small business) and
partially wrong (nurture and legitimacy are real jobs) `MD5-HByRxoA`.

**Rates, stated at different times.** Treat as a progression and an order of
magnitude, never as a target:

| Rate | Where |
|---|---|
| 7 posts/week → **80/week**; audience 180K→1.2M, growth *rate* up ~10x | `WrCt0R3FBFs` |
| **~160/week** across all platforms, two people | `MD5-HByRxoA` |
| **250/week** — "1,000 a month, so 12,000 a year" | `60_7PU9JDIw` |
| **Rule of 100**: 100 primary actions/day for outbound and paid; **100/week for organic content** | `WrCt0R3FBFs` |

And the horizon, which is the number clients most need to hear: **~18 months**
of content for Acquisition.com before any deal flow `MD5-HByRxoA`; a podcast
started in 2017 that first hit the entrepreneurship top 10 five and a half to six
years later, never missing a week `7ITff1fIbSc`; **400 long-form pieces** as the
unit of work, with "as long as it takes" as the honest schedule `7ITff1fIbSc`.

> "You need to be able to do the doing without seeing the result of your doing."

Which is why he sets the **activity** as the goal, not the outcome `7ITff1fIbSc`:
did I make the post, did I do the reach-outs. Income can swing without your
self-assessment moving — and that is what survives the flat years.

## The production line — treat it as manufacturing

`ZTSI3DDP_4A`

> "It's a manufacturing process. It's digital manufacturing."

Raw input: recorded video. Output: posts that generate views, likes, shares and
leads. Once it is manufacturing, every manufacturing question is available — cost
per unit, cycle time, which steps can be deleted.

### The Algorithm, in strict order `ZTSI3DDP_4A` ⚠ contrarian

Musk's five steps: **question → delete → simplify → accelerate → automate.**

> "The only thing that is more efficient than automation is elimination."

**The order is the whole value.** Because AI is now so easy, everyone starts at
step five and automates work that should not be done at all. His rule of thumb on
step two, borrowed from Musk: delete so much you have to add stuff back. People
fear deletion; in a *process* (unlike a machine) it is a reversible door.

The entry point into step one is three questions of his own:

> "What does that mean? How do you know that? So what?"

Worked live on his own pipeline: *"we have to colour grade."* What does that
mean? — all this work on the video. So what? — any evidence it does anything? No.
Who decided it was required? *"What god on the throne of YouTube and Instagram
said that colour grading was super important?"* Nobody. **It came out.**

Then the simplify arithmetic, granting a benefit for the sake of argument: 4 of
the 10 hours were colour grading. Is that 40% of the output's quality? No. 5%?
Maybe. Worth 40% of the cost? No — **because of opportunity cost**, since the team
has plenty else to edit.

> "It would make more sense for us to do two videos or one and a half videos at
> 95% than one video at 100."

**The opportunity-cost clause is load-bearing:** the answer flips if the
production team is idle.

### Production value tested flat, twice

| Finding | Where |
|---|---|
| A stage, lighting, seating and slides performed **the same** as him talking to a red camera with slides, or a plain whiteboard | `JOfsujnXxCg` |
| A video shot **on an iPhone on a plane** — filmed that way because he was annoyed at his team's production requirements — became a top-decile performer | `ZTSI3DDP_4A` |

> "The content is the container, right? The stuff inside is what matters."

Read it precisely: this is evidence for deleting **production** steps, not for
deleting effort on **substance**. The point is where the effort got reallocated.

### The cheaper content is the deeper content `JOfsujnXxCg` ⚠ contrarian

Narrower education raised his **total** views despite fewer views per video,
and the mechanism is production cost: education needs far less post-editing, and
it is what he does every day anyway, so the team ships more of it. The audience
preferred it too — people trying to learn were happier without the whiz-bangs.

The internal target has a name: **qualium** `ZTSI3DDP_4A` — quality volume. And
the resolution is not a compromise on quality per piece; it is a **reduction in
cost per piece**.

### Diagnostics for the line

- **How much actual time are you working on the thing?** `ZTSI3DDP_4A` Compare
  time-on-task against calendar elapsed time. If the work is 10 hours and delivery
  takes a week, everything outside those 10 hours is waste — uncaptured potential
  value. It measures latency, not effort.
- **End of day instead of end of week.** `ZTSI3DDP_4A` `WrCt0R3FBFs` He puts the
  team multiplier at **7x** flatly, and calls the seven-week project that finishes
  in days the same arithmetic. Make end-of-day the default for all activities
  unless stated otherwise. (His number, his assertion — it is the ratio of cycle
  lengths, not a study.)
- **Though I am using AI, are we getting more?** `ZTSI3DDP_4A` ⚠ contrarian Three
  parallel questions: more views, more leads, more money. If token costs rise
  month over month and revenue does not, go back to **step one**, not to the
  prompt — you probably automated something that should not exist.

## Repurposing and the test bed

`WrCt0R3FBFs` — his five-step content model:

1. **Test first** on a platform that tolerates high frequency. He moved his
   private me-to-me idea dump onto X at the same time cost, which made every note
   public and testable. X and TikTok take ~10 posts/day without hurting the
   account; Instagram audiences fatigue at about one.
2. **Record the winners as video.**
   > "So tweets turn into videos, the threads turn into long videos, the short
   > tweets turn into shorts."
3. **Add CTAs into the recordings.**
4. **Contextualise the thumbnail and text per platform** — do not repost the same
   file.
5. **Distribute at volume.**

He notes the packaging skill itself — phase three into four — takes about **six
months** to learn even when you can pay for help `MD5-HByRxoA`.

**Thirty call-to-actions** `WrCt0R3FBFs`. List every ask you would ever make (buy
the book, leave a review, tag me, share with a friend, register, grab the lead
magnet), write **five variants of each** — about thirty — each running 5–10
seconds rather than three minutes, placed at the beginning, middle and end. He
credits adding a review-and-share ask for taking the podcast from **20,000 to
400,000 downloads a month**.

**Kaleidoscope the winners** `reisEL_D7xc`. When something wins, remake it ten
ways rather than hunt the next one: background/backdrop colour, different props,
literal re-enactment, reorder, filters, effects, fonts and captions, pacing,
music, same hook against a different body.

**Organic is the cheap test bed for the expensive channel** `MD5-HByRxoA`. Watch
for content people disproportionately save and share; port the winning hooks into
paid ads and cold email and split-test against control.

And on the ask itself: **one plain CTA, value the rest of the time**
`MD5-HByRxoA` ⚠ contrarian. People pitch too hard against the small demand they
have, which is what stops the audience ever growing.

## Proof engineering — the part that is actually his edge

`XsWSvz-aewA` `ZTSI3DDP_4A`

> "We're in the show-me times, not the tell-me times."

**Proof splits in two** `XsWSvz-aewA`: **third-party proof** is the record —
exits, revenue, headcount, number of companies — stateable once and static.
**Demonstration proof** is people watching you use your expertise in real time,
which is *"incredibly difficult to fake"* and is renewable.

**The question he asks of his own calendar** — not his content calendar:

> "How can I feed the proof machine?"

The test of a good answer is that it **adds no extra recording time**.

**The self-licking ice cream cone** `XsWSvz-aewA`: engineer proof generation
inside the way you already service customers, so selling produces marketing and
marketing produces selling. His hair-extension example: put sweepstakes tickets
inside the product, then call winners personally and install the extensions in
person. Sell → customers come in → they buy → that generates marketing → that
brings customers.

**If you do not have enough customers to generate proof** `XsWSvz-aewA`: do the
work for free, publicly, and document it.

> "Let me do a whole bunch of work for free, I'll document it, and I'll just give
> it away. Who says no to that?"

**And the systems version** `mr4Pw66_490`: seven installable processes that make
marketing arrive at you — a weekly ritual screenshotting praise; incentives for
testimonials; lifecycle ads assembled from recordings you already make; capturing
the emotive client moment at delivery; a bounty per support-chat testimonial;
turning existing Google/Yelp reviews into marketing; the one-star treatment. Each
runs on material the business already produces. **Install them before you remove
the founder from camera, not after.**

Why it matters more now: the AI threat to a creator is a function of **how much
risk their content asks the consumer to take**, not how good the content is
`XsWSvz-aewA`. Entertainment gets disrupted first; B2B, where acting on the advice
is expensive, last. And he is clear the protection is temporary.

## The economics of production

| Figure | Where |
|---|---|
| **~160 pieces/week across all platforms for two people costs ~$70,000/month** | `MD5-HByRxoA` |
| Buying the equivalent impressions would cost **~$2M/month** — so the organic route is an arbitrage worth roughly that difference, and the lever on whether it works is how good the content is | `MD5-HByRxoA` |
| **250 pieces/week** against **three or four asks a year** to the list — the ratio is the point | `60_7PU9JDIw` |
| **Vendors first, then in-house**: platform specialists get you up to speed ~**5x faster**, then you teach employees what the vendors taught you | `MD5-HByRxoA` |
| **Hire someone native to the platform** — a twenty-year-old who lives there — for instant *diagnosis*, not production: the hook is off, you're trying to look professional when it needs to be iPhone selfie style | `MH-IMJxbUY4` |

> "If I were to just pay for the impressions, it would cost me about 2 million a
> month."

**The $70K/month figure is his cost at his scale with vendors and staff.** He
offers it as the price of using content instead of paid ads, explicitly not as a
small-business budget.

## ⚠ What this card does NOT know — hooks, thumbnails and titles

**Read this before briefing anyone on packaging.** The blind spot is real but it
is narrower than "he never covers it," and getting the boundary right matters.

**What the corpus DOES have, for organic content:**

- **Vague to clear** `Jmkq5RLjm0U` `HVOubeXUcx0` ⚠ contrarian — say exactly what
  the thing is in the thumbnail and headline instead of manufacturing curiosity.
  He names the marketing narrative he is rejecting and rejects it for education
  content, conceding entertainment plays by different rules.
  > "if they want that thing, they will click on that thing, and then you just
  > give them that thing"
- **Assume nothing** `Jmkq5RLjm0U` `HVOubeXUcx0` — retitle for strangers: *"the
  Alex Hormozi guide to haters"* becomes *"business influencer crushes haters and
  shows how you can too"*; *"day in the life of Alex Hormozi"* becomes *"day in
  the life of a $200 million per year CEO."*
- **Match the thumbnail to the first five seconds** `5JLqmQlGG0U` — confirm the
  packaging in the opening five seconds or the video underperforms regardless of
  quality. He split-tests thumbnails against **two** metrics — clicks, and how
  long the people who clicked *that* thumbnail then watch — and reports **20–30%
  swings in watch time** from framing alone.
  > "You didn't match the thumbnail to the first 5 seconds. You didn't confirm
  > it."
- **Proof, Promise, Plan** `Jmkq5RLjm0U` — derived by rewatching his top 35
  educational videos. Entertainment intros confirm the thumbnail; **education
  intros prove the teacher.**
- **There is no such thing as too long, only too boring** `MD5-HByRxoA`.

**What the corpus genuinely does NOT have:**

1. **No hook formulas for organic content.** All the hook craft in the corpus
   sits in the **paid-ads** lane — see [`channel-paid-ads.md`](channel-paid-ads.md):
   writing 50 hooks in a weekly session with 40 of them tried-and-true
   `reisEL_D7xc`; modelling the first three seconds of winners visually and
   verbally `JDR-R--4HhM`; spending 80% of creative resources reskinning winners
   `fSbqaTlWaYI`; the headline as 80 cents of the advertising dollar
   `JDR-R--4HhM`. The one bridge he states is directional and runs the *wrong
   way* for us — organic winners become **paid** hooks `MD5-HByRxoA` — so the
   paid material is a plausible source of transferable technique, but he never
   says so.
2. **No thumbnail design craft.** He says match it, test it, and make it clear.
   Nothing on composition, faces, colour, text count or contrast.
3. **No title templates.** The `Assume nothing` rewrites are the only worked
   examples, and they teach one principle (name the stranger's stake), not a
   pattern library.
4. **No per-platform specifications** beyond post-frequency tolerances and "hire
   a native."

**So Rex's honest position:** he can argue *packaging clarity* and *proof-led
intros* from the corpus with citations, and he can carry the paid-side hook
discipline across as an explicitly-labelled analogy. He **cannot** claim Hormozi
doctrine on hook writing, thumbnail design or title formulas for organic content,
because there is none. Say so rather than improvise — a fabricated formula
attributed to the corpus is the exact failure the
[`README`](README.md) was built to prevent.

## Rex's read for our three clients

- **industries** — the welding shop is phase zero, and phase **two** is the whole
  ambition: one platform, a cadence it can actually hold, aimed at nurture rather
  than lead gen, so a contractor who found us another way checks the profile and
  concludes we are real and still in business. Capture, do not create: the work is
  already visual, and the proof machine is fed by photographing what leaves the
  shop. Never brief a `$70K/month` production idea at a business with no marketing
  function — the 18-month lag alone disqualifies content as its acquisition
  channel.
- **turf-monster** — we are posting to X and TikTok with **no performance
  read-back**, which means we are at phase three volume on a phase-one
  measurement, and every one of this card's diagnostics needs a number we do not
  have. Install the read-back **before** touching cadence. Then the two cheapest
  wins are structural, not creative: X as the **test bed** (it tolerates ~10/day
  and tells us what works before we film it), and the **thirty CTAs** — the app
  has at least six distinct asks and is almost certainly repeating one. And the
  customer-questions play is sitting right there unused: every support message
  about the wallet step is a post nobody has written.
- **operator-brand** — this card is the studio's channel, and the strong story is
  exactly the raw input the system wants. The `eVY4Zo-hpqU` origination rule fits
  the work by construction — every shipped task is a fresh real conversation with
  an outcome — and `XsWSvz-aewA`'s "capture, don't create" means the content cost
  is near zero because the artefacts already exist. Hold two lines hard: **scope
  every claim to what we actually observed** (the seven-clients phrasing), and
  **"how I," never "how to."** The honest expectation to set is 400 pieces and a
  multi-year clock, with the activity as the goal — not a 90-day lead-gen mandate,
  which he treats as the standard wrong ask.
