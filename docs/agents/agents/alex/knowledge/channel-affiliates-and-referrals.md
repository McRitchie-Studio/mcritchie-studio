## The claim

**There are two people who can promote your thing: you, and everybody else.**
Everybody else is a larger group. That is the whole argument, and he states it
that flatly `60_7PU9JDIw`.

The channel is not a bonus on top of marketing. Three of his businesses ran on it
as the *primary* acquisition channel: Skool (majority of traffic is people telling
people), Allan (almost entirely affiliates), Prestige Labs (almost 100% affiliate
sales through brick-and-mortar distributors) `DaRXece2ItE`.

> "There's two people who can promote what you have. You and other people. And
> there are more of them than there are of you."

## The definition that decides everything

**An affiliate is an independent business that tells its own audience to buy your
stuff** `DaRXece2ItE`. Two clauses do the work:

1. They own their own business and **do their own advertising** — so they bring
   their own traffic.
2. They agree to offer your stuff to their engaged leads **in exchange for money,
   free stuff, or both**.

> "They're an independent business that tells their audience to buy your stuff."

A **referral** passes along a friend. An **affiliate** brings a crowd. From the
outside they look identical; underneath they are different, which is why an
affiliate behaves like an advertising channel you have to *sell to* rather than a
favour you have to *ask for* `DaRXece2ItE`.

**And the industry name is noise.** Physical products say *influencer*.
Customer-made versions say *UGC*. Direct response says *affiliate*. Brick and
mortar says *referral*. Finance says *center of influence* or *COI*. Elsewhere it
is a *joint venture*. All of them are people who tell other people about your
stuff for money, free stuff, or love of the product `DaRXece2ItE`. Use this when a
client says their industry has no affiliate channel — it usually has one under a
different word.

## The mechanism

### The gate: worth it, know about it, easy `DaRXece2ItE`

Not a checklist — a gate. Miss any one and the program does not work.

| Leg | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Make it worth it** | The offer *to the affiliate*. Money, free stuff, or both |
| **Let them know about it** | A dedicated affiliate sales page, plus promotion pointed at it |
| **Make it easy** | Onboarding, the tracked link, the leaderboard, ready-made collateral |

He adds a fourth beat running underneath all three — **follow-up** — on the
grounds that you cannot expect people to magically promote your stuff.

> "You got to make it worth it for them. You got to let them know about it. And
> you got to make it easy."

### Start with the offer, because the affiliate is a customer `DaRXece2ItE`

Just as a business starts with an offer, an affiliate program starts with the
offer you make the affiliate. He spent **two full days** purely on permutations of
the prize structure and calls offer design the highest-leverage thing you can do.
The finished thing looks obvious, which is the trap: *"it always seems obvious in
retrospect."*

And you now have **two customers and two offers** `60_7PU9JDIw` — the offer you
make the affiliate, and the offer the affiliate makes on your behalf. That means
two concurrent campaigns for the whole window: one to the people you want buying,
one to the affiliates reminding them to keep promoting. He is explicit that
signing an affiliate up is the easy part and getting them to actually advertise is
the hard part.

### Win-win-win — the rule most people miss `60_7PU9JDIw` `DaRXece2ItE` ⚠ contrarian

Paying the affiliate aligns *your* incentive with *theirs* and leaves the
audience out. The affiliate is then spending goodwill with their own people to
earn your commission — a withdrawal. So they hold back, because they have to save
that goodwill for their own offers.

**The fix is to design the prize so the affiliate's audience gets it.** His worked
contrast: "the top 10 affiliates get a live AMA delivered to their audience"
rather than "the top 10 affiliates get supercars." The supercar version makes
their email read *help me win my contest*; the AMA version reads *I want to
support you*.

> "If you can show them that their audience wins by them associating with you, you
> will unlock the doors."

The sharpest version of this makes the prize **generate the traffic itself**
`DaRXece2ItE`: the fireside-chat prize works because the affiliate's audience must
click the affiliate's link to submit questions, which the affiliate then asks on
their behalf. Someone in the ATM niche gets his big-business thinking applied to
ATMs — an answer available nowhere else.

### Minimum activation point — scale the bar to the friction `DaRXece2ItE`

The qualification threshold is **set by how hard the action is**, not by how much
you want.

| Action required | Minimum |
|---|---|
| Bring a person **in person** to your facility (e.g. a gym) | **2** — because that is a lot of work |
| A **text introduction** | **4 or 5** |
| An **opt-in** | **10+** — the friction is so low the bar has to rise |

> "if it's just can you get someone to opt into something I have to make that
> higher because the friction is so low"

He ran 10+ opt-ins the previous year, it worked, so he ran it again. The 10-person
threshold is corroborated in the earlier launch, where anyone clearing 10
registrations won two unreleased bonus chapters `60_7PU9JDIw`.

**This is the bottom rung, not the target.** Its job is to give the small promoter
something reachable.

### Two tiers, because you want the whales *and* the longtail

He refuses the either-or: you want the person who can bring 10,000 and the person
who can bring three `DaRXece2ItE`.

> "There's scarcity at the top, but then just a tiny hurdle for everyone."

The reason for the bottom tier is stated mechanically `60_7PU9JDIw`: somebody with
a 100-person email list looks at a top-10 leaderboard, concludes they can never
win, and **does nothing**.

The structure he built has an **unscalable** top prize and a ladder of cheaper
approximations beneath it `DaRXece2ItE`:

| Rung | Prize |
|---|---|
| Top 10 | In-person fireside 1:1 Q&A, plus a dinner |
| Top 50 | Three hours of Q&A at his HQ, a shot at their question, and meeting each other |
| Everyone over the minimum | An audiobook, a raffle for 50 of those seats, and the recording |

Each rung is a cheaper approximation of the rung above. The top prize must be
something you genuinely *cannot* scale, or the scarcity is fake.

### The friction dial — one decision that sets the whole program `60_7PU9JDIw`

> "If you want volume, you make it easier. If you want quality, you make it
> harder."

- **High friction** (tax paperwork, the 1099) is a **feature in B2B**: anyone who
  fills it out is far more likely to actually promote, and it lets you qualify who
  is sending you leads — which matters most when you sell services.
- **Low friction** for a cheap, mass-market product delivered without your time:
  he stripped the form to first name, last name, email, phone — a **30-second
  signup** — accepting worse affiliates to get scale.

He runs the value equation on the signup step itself: value = becoming an
affiliate; time delay = 30 seconds; effort = typing your details; likelihood =
they click submit and it's done. Someone can sign up in 30 seconds and be posting
a story immediately.

### Onboarding: the link, the leaderboard, the collateral

| Move | Why |
|---|---|
| **Issue the tracked link immediately** `DaRXece2ItE` | He names the objection first: if they do not believe your attribution is good, they will not send traffic they cannot see counted |
| **Deliver that link twice** — thank-you page *and* email `60_7PU9JDIw` | People lose it. He puts the lift at **~25%** more affiliates who actually promote |
| **Live public leaderboard** `DaRXece2ItE` | Proves attribution in real time; competitors check it daily. Send it as frequently as possible |
| **Two emails a week** `60_7PU9JDIw` | One of usable creative, one of *where you stand*. Promise the cadence on day one and keep it exactly. Most people skip the standings half, and affiliates constantly ask "where am I?" |
| **Hand them collateral that already converted** `DaRXece2ItE` | Pre-written, pre-approved emails, posts, ads and texts they can drop into Slack, WhatsApp and group threads. He calls the package the *blackbooks* |
| **Segment the collateral by promoter type** `DaRXece2ItE` | Warm-audience video people (IG/YouTube); text people (Substack, X, LinkedIn, blogs); **pure paid-media operators with no audience at all** — some of his top performers were the third kind, who just ran his proven ads |
| **Hold a second packet back ~3 weeks in** `60_7PU9JDIw` | Guarantees their creative does not fatigue mid-campaign, and the withheld material reads as insider intel, which is what makes their audience click *their* link rather than going direct |
| **Ship the winners mid-campaign** `DaRXece2ItE` | A "here are the winners" drop of the ads, posts, angles and hooks outperforming right now, so they can use your data to win for their own audience |
| **Call the top of the leaderboard** `DaRXece2ItE` | His marketing director personally reaches out to climbers: "if you crush it, we want to help you crush it even more" |
| **Share live campaign performance as the perk** `DaRXece2ItE` | Marketers want to see what is working more than they want a kickback. Normally unshareable data; he calls it one of the most underrated components |

> "if you expect them to make an entire campaign to launch your thing, good luck.
> It's not going to happen"

> "as soon as there's friction, they'll stop"

### Recruiting them — the Core Four, pointed inward `DaRXece2ItE`

He runs warm outreach, cold outreach, email, content and ads **to recruit
affiliates**, then teaches the affiliates to run the same four for him. Affiliates
are the multiplier on the Core Four, not a fifth channel. (See
[`core-four.md`](core-four.md).)

Two recruiting plays worth copying exactly:

- **The thank-you page** `DaRXece2ItE`. After someone buys or registers, a button
  at the bottom: *do you want to send anybody else, or do you have an audience —
  would you like to get more free prizes?* It converts the highest-intent moment
  in the funnel into affiliate recruitment at zero media cost.
- **Two emails to your own list, then stop** `DaRXece2ItE`. A plain FYI and an ICYMI.
  Then all focus back on the launch itself. Last year's affiliates are a separate,
  small, already-opted-in segment he will email one or two more times.
  > "I want to promote the book, not promote promoting the book"

**Do not confuse the two lists.** The affiliate list gets two emails a week
because it opted into that frequency. The **customer** list gets **two, maximum
three promotional asks over the entire campaign** `60_7PU9JDIw` ⚠ contrarian — and
write those assuming customers will read them, never framing the promotion in
sales terms like "we're going to sell a ton of books here."

### The page, and the one test on it

Build a page whose only job is selling people on becoming an affiliate
`DaRXece2ItE`: each prize, exactly what they have to do, and how you will help
them do it. Opt in with name and email, receive the custom link. The page has a
different name in every industry — influencer page, partner page, center of
influence page, JV page — and the name does not matter as long as the page exists.

One measured test on it: **changing the banner above the submit form raised
affiliate signups by 60%** `60_7PU9JDIw`. He offers it as evidence that page-level
details still pay at scale, not as an expected lift.

## Referrals — the local, no-audience version

Everything above assumes the promoter has a crowd. When they do not, the plays
change shape.

### Ride the product, not the goodwill — BAM FAM `B-ogfFiQpXg`

Book a meeting from a meeting. At every appointment, **do not ask for a referral —
ask who they want to bring next time.**

> "now we're not asking for referrals. We're just saying who do you want to bring"

What makes it natural is a benefit built into the membership: five guest
treatments as a member perk, framed on the evidence that people who do healthy
things together succeed more. The ask rides on the *product* rather than on
goodwill, the marginal cost of a guest is near zero, and every touch point becomes
a lead source. He treats this as the single change doing most of the work in that
business.

### Put the ask at the wow moment `B-ogfFiQpXg`

Find the point in the journey where results **reliably** land — a universal one,
not a per-customer one. He pushed until they agreed most patients feel
dramatically better by **day 90**, which fixed the cadence: one ask at close as
part of the offer, a second at the 90-day visit when the results are undeniable.

### Make the referral socially positive `B-ogfFiQpXg` ⚠ contrarian

A $500 credit to a customer paying $5K–$20K a year makes no difference — the *ask*
produced the referrals, not the credit. Replace it with something that makes the
referrer look good: five $1,000–$2,000 treatments they can hand to friends, at a
fraction of that in real cost. Keep it exclusive to referrals so you are not
discounting to strangers.

This is the same win-win-win rule in a local business: **give them something
valuable to give away**, because a referrer is staking their own reputation
`KhFlD54nQrY` — when you recommend a movie and the friend hates it, your judgement
is discounted forever after. A bad experience by a referred customer damages two
brands at once.

> "that person who made the referral risks relational capital. They risk their
> personal brand to the friend."

### Giveaways as referral mechanics `B-ogfFiQpXg`

Quarterly drawing, grand prize is **a full year of the top-tier service** (~$20K),
and **everyone who enters gets something regardless** — so the referrer is doing
their friend a favour by entering them.

Two mechanisms he names, and they are not the obvious ones:

1. A giveaway lets you **state the price openly**, which removes sticker shock
   later when non-winners are offered the $5K or $10K tier.
2. It is **not a pressured sale**.

His answer to the fear of giving away a $20K client: you have 475 patients, one
will not move the business.

> "this is marketing. We have to be willing to give things away"

Distribution is cheap: a **PS at the bottom of the newsletter** `B-ogfFiQpXg`,
which keeps a low-pressure asset low-pressure while converting it into a lead
source.

### Two local partner plays with no commission at all `60_7PU9JDIw`

- **The employer gift.** A massage therapist finds a local employer hiring ~10
  people a week and offers a coupon for two free massages in every new-employee
  packet. No commission. The employer's payoff is reputational — happier, less
  stressed employees, a better onboarding experience. Do it with 10 or 100
  companies and the books fill; the only cost is the outreach.
  > "Why would that business say no to giving their employees some sort of treat?
  > And how much did that cost me in advertising?"
- **The adjacent-service partner.** Find the business that sees your customer
  *immediately before* they need you — a chiropractor for a massage therapist —
  and give their customers your service free. A chiropractor taking 50 new
  patients a month sends a percentage over.

### Sell the partner a real offer instead of a swap `OQf2Ba-Lp_4`

Let the partner sell **your** lead magnet and keep 100% of the cash, doing no
work. You deliver it at hard cost and upsell your core offer on site. CAC = cost
of delivering the lead magnet ÷ conversion from lead magnet to core offer. On real
numbers: $100 hard cost at a conservative 50% conversion is a **$200 CAC against a
$3–4K job**.

The pitch is real money, no extra work, and **no obligation for you to
reciprocate**.

### And the leftover traffic `Q2VUuUGfpNY`

Everyone who reaches your sales process and cannot afford you is paid-for traffic
you are throwing away. Downsell them — including **to somebody else's product**,
taking affiliate or referral commissions. You make money, they get free customers,
the customer's need gets solved.

## When it applies

**It is the primary channel when your buyer already gathers behind somebody
else.** It is a supplement when they do not — a $6.4M travel business got only ~5%
of customers from affiliates and charity partners `MH-IMJxbUY4`, and that was fine
because the constraint was elsewhere.

**It rewards small and connected markets.** The smaller the niche and the more
local the market, the more word of mouth matters, and that is where the absurd
returns come from `KhFlD54nQrY`.

**Do not open it as your sixth channel.** A trash business losing $151K was
running door-to-door, Meta, cold email, handwritten letters *and* referrals; the
prescription cut to **one** `Wr6n_zNKvMk`. And if a handful of referral partners
already feed you, the problem is dependency, not volume — go quote work for their
competitors `0EqJD2o-Mnk`.

> "Right now you're just dependent on them. So they have all the leverage. So you
> need more wells."

**Know what you are building.** Affiliate revenue is the fastest and lowest-risk
money for both parties — nobody pays until a sale happens, and you are uncapped —
and it has **almost no exit value** `oRqnTOT9ZG8`. The cash flow is worth a
multiple discounted by the durability of the counterparty, so an affiliate stream
riding somebody's temporary arbitrage is worth roughly nothing.

**One structural note.** Affiliates hold you to a *higher* standard for how you
treat their customers than you hold yourself for your own `60_7PU9JDIw`, because
everyone believes they treat customers better than they do. He calls the pressure
a benefit and credits it with messaging changes he would never otherwise have made.

## Rex's read for our three clients

- **industries** — the welding shop is the `60_7PU9JDIw` local case almost
  exactly, and the two no-commission plays are the ones to run: find the general
  contractors and facilities managers who see our customer *immediately before*
  they need a weld, and give their customers something free. It costs outreach,
  not margin. But note the delivery ceiling — a referral engine that fills a
  booked-out shop buys nothing, so this waits behind capacity or gets aimed at
  higher-ticket work. And the minimum activation point here is **2**, because
  bringing a job in person is real work.
- **turf-monster** — a pick'em app is the one client where the affiliate
  arithmetic actually closes: the product is cheap, mass-market and delivered
  without anyone's time, which is exactly the low-friction end of the dial. Run
  the 30-second signup, the tracked link delivered **twice**, the leaderboard
  (which this product already knows how to render), and a minimum of **10+**
  because an app signup is a low-friction action. The prize must benefit the
  *invitee*, not the inviter — a free entry for the friend, not a payout to the
  referrer — or we are asking people to spend goodwill on us. Hard blocker to fix
  first: **the crypto-wallet onboarding step is friction on the affiliate's
  audience**, and "as soon as there's friction, they'll stop" applies to their
  people, not just to them.
- **operator-brand** — the studio has no audience, so it has no affiliates; it has
  the `Z2tgZC_XkT4` problem instead, which is a different card. What *does* apply
  today is the thank-you-page recruit and the missionaries argument: the strong
  story is the only currency we have, and it pays in association rather than cash.
  Realistically this channel is **premature** — it needs either goodwill we have
  not banked or commission on a product we are not yet selling at volume. Park it
  behind content.
