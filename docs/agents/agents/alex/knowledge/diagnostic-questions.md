## The claim

He does not prescribe before he has the numbers, and the questions come in an
order. The order is not stylistic — asking the money question before the capacity
question is how you sell a growth plan to a business that cannot deliver it.

This is Rex's intake. Run it top to bottom.

## The mechanism

### 0. Frame — before any numbers

| Question | What it is really testing |
|---|---|
| **What problem are we solving?** `N5MExtki_VI` | He asks it whenever someone dumps information on him. The follow-on is immediate: can we solve this same problem without a new product, channel or business? It is a filter against solving a constraint by adding surface area |
| **What would stop me from 10x-ing the business right now?** `reisEL_D7xc` | If nothing, 10x it now. If something, that thing is the constraint — isolate it, fix it, push volume, repeat at the next bottleneck |
| **What's the one thing that if it were true, all my other problems would go away?** `KhFlD54nQrY` | Forces one priority instead of twenty urgent, unimportant ones |
| **Is that actually the constraint, or the thing you enjoy?** `KhFlD54nQrY` | Owners ask about the function they love, which is by definition the function that is not their problem. A sales guy asking for the closer framework at a 40% close rate is not asking about his constraint |
| **Have you been a customer of your own product?** `KhFlD54nQrY` | The ego question. Usually the answer is no, not recently, followed by "but I don't think that's the problem." The restaurant is not stalled on SEO; the food is mediocre |

### 1. Capacity — can you take the demand you are asking me for?

Ask this before prescribing any marketing at all. If the business is supply
constrained, more leads make the real problem worse.

| Question | What it is really testing |
|---|---|
| **If we double the lead flow of the business, can you handle that?** `OQf2Ba-Lp_4` | The first problem to delineate in every business. Yes → demand constrained, go to work on marketing. No → build resources first |
| **If you double the amount of customers you have, does it break anything?** `j2TZMFkj71Q` | The same test, asked before any analysis. A no scopes the whole session to offer and traffic |
| **Can you handle 20 people a week?** `BYpTRiRqS1Y` | He deliberately rephrases mid-sentence from *could you sell* to *can you handle* — the two answers differ, and only the second one is capacity |
| **What breaks when we do more?** `0EqJD2o-Mnk` | Then: what stops you from fixing that? He rules out the easy answers first — you are motivated, you know how, you know it is now — so something is blocking you. Then he presses the wording: *have to or choose to*, *can't or won't* |
| **If you got half your time back, could you double the business?** `0EqJD2o-Mnk` | For the founder who is the bottleneck and resisting a hire. A yes ends the debate: the hire pays for itself by definition |
| **Can you take three months off and have it grow anyway?** `mr4Pw66_490` | The final test. The passing grade is growth, not survival. He says actually take the ninety days, expect something to break, and repeat until nothing does |

### 2. Money — what is a customer worth, and what does one cost?

| Question | What it is really testing |
|---|---|
| **What's a customer worth to you over 30 days? What's a customer cost me?** `i7bLRKwKSms` | Two numbers, in that order. The second means cost to **acquire** — marketing, advertising, content, sales commissions — never cost to deliver |
| **How do you get customers? Walk me through the money stuff.** `o64cI6tebnU` | The money probe drills to average ticket and then to **gross profit per ticket per revenue stream**, not to revenue |
| **What's sales velocity? How many do you sell a month?** `N5MExtki_VI` | Converts any business description into a monthly unit count, then walks the funnel backwards from it: registrants, attendees, calls booked, calls held, sales, client count, churn |
| **What do you charge?** `qsXxckCbci0` | His single north star for how far along a service business is. Do a good job → become supply constrained → raise price → raise margin → repeat |
| **If for every dollar you gave me, I gave you $5 back, what would your budget be?** `OQf2Ba-Lp_4` | Breaks the owner's emotional ceiling on ad spend. The only correct answer is as many as possible, then recycle the returns |
| **Why aren't we spending 50,000 a day?** `KhFlD54nQrY` | The objections map straight to diagnoses. *ROAS drops when we spend more* → your ads suck. *We can't handle the call volume* → you can't recruit and train. *We couldn't deliver* → systematize the back end |
| **What stops you from just spending $70,000?** `Wr6n_zNKvMk` | Same move on a business spending $7,500 total at 20:1. The block turned out to be cash timing, not demand — which is what produced the offer work |
| **Why don't you spend up to $100 CPA?** `j2TZMFkj71Q` | For a profitable operator who capped spend "for no good reason." He separates cadence of spending from amount, and treats the amount as a decision |
| **How many people show up to your thing?** `BYpTRiRqS1Y` | *My market cannot afford it* is usually a sample-size problem. 200 people a year, all word of mouth, is a doubly biased sample. Look at 2,000 |

### 3. Funnel — where does the flow break?

| Question | What it is really testing |
|---|---|
| **Are your conversions hit or miss?** `3yAiVjcImQ4` | If you cannot say why they vary, the missing thing is a **sales flow**, not a better closer. Founder-led selling that works is exactly what makes nothing repeatable |
| **Why should I listen to you?** `HVOubeXUcx0` | The cold viewer's first question, and it blocks everything until answered. He calls it a sign above their head before they turn on the volume of their brain |
| **What wow moment are people like *whoa*?** `B-ogfFiQpXg` | Find the point where results reliably land — he pushed until they agreed on day 90 — then put the referral ask there |
| **If I were to delete everything, what one thing would you fight for?** `-j8_YCWZ05Q` | Paired with: if I removed only one feature, which would you not mind losing. Chart both and the one or two things carrying the product fall out |
| **What are your EPCs?** `j2TZMFkj71Q` | Earnings per click — the one metric missing from a dashboard that already had CPA, AOV, conversion and LTV |
| **How many of your interests does one audience member share?** `5JLqmQlGG0U` | Bond strength in a personal brand. Narrowing the declared interest set narrows the audience and deepens the bond |

### 4. Attribution — do the numbers mean anything?

| Question | What it is really testing |
|---|---|
| **Does the reported ratio survive arithmetic?** `HGZOxBfnF-E` | A reported 0.41:1 LTV to CAC would mean losing money on 80% of revenue, which was not happening. So the number is false. His alternative explanation is worse: referral and repeat sales credited to ads, money burning out the back. Either way there is no basis to spend more **or** less until attribution is fixed |
| **What metrics do you track?** `mr4Pw66_490` | He grades skill in any field by the quality and quantity of metrics volunteered. The first good HR hire interviewed *him*: cost of acquiring talent, time to fill, two-way fit at 90 days. He knew none of them |
| **How do you in your role make the company money?** `mr4Pw66_490` | Asked of every non-revenue role. "I hold the camera" is refused — marketing makes money, but you don't do marketing, you hold the camera, so how does that make us money? |
| **Did the pairing net you more money?** `VQM3DrnVTcs` | Settles campaign and partnership arguments. Not an opinion — measured against the **ideal** customer's response, not the general public's |
| **Do the right people text you?** `dMZ-n2KSlxE` | His own top signal: business owners he likes and respects texting that a video was fire. Contrarian — it outranks platform metrics |
| **Are you making content for your team or your avatar?** `JOfsujnXxCg` | He made videos his team suggested and did not notice for months. A $10M friend said he guessed he was not the avatar — and he was, exactly |

### 5. When the owner is the constraint

- **Think out the worst case in specificity** `_KlZoPxbStk` — the fears on the
  downside are suffering, not truth, and people refuse to specify them. His own
  floor was sleeping on a gym turf floor, which also became steady state.
- **Treat the owner's stated constraint as a data point, not the answer.** On
  `o64cI6tebnU` he asks "what's stopping you right now" **last**, and then
  overrode the answer: the owner said salespeople; he prescribed a product change.

## The counter-intuitive readings

Learn these five, because the naive read points the opposite way.

| Signal | The naive read | His read |
|---|---|---|
| Exceptionally good close rate `3yAiVjcImQ4` `OQf2Ba-Lp_4` | Great sales team | **You are underpriced.** At or above 80% he pencils a raise on the spot |
| A ratio that says you should be losing money, while you are profitable `HGZOxBfnF-E` | The business is broken | **The data is broken.** Fix attribution before changing spend in either direction |
| "My market can't afford it" `BYpTRiRqS1Y` | Wrong market, lower the price | **Sample size.** You have seen 200 people, all by word of mouth |
| Inconsistent conversions `3yAiVjcImQ4` | Need a better closer | **No sales flow.** Nothing is recorded, scripted or improvable |
| Supply vs. demand constrained `OQf2Ba-Lp_4` `j2TZMFkj71Q` `BYpTRiRqS1Y` | More leads is always good | Owners routinely fix a problem whose solution makes the real problem worse |

## Where the corpus disagrees with itself

**The comment section.** In `JOfsujnXxCg` he blames himself for not reading the
comments — business owners had been telling him what they wanted and he "just
wasn't listening"; correcting it lifted comments per view 25%. In `Jmkq5RLjm0U`
he tells the opposite story: the comments **led him astray**, because people
loved the styles he was making while the owners he could actually help had
stopped watching. Same signal, opposite verdicts. The reconciling line is his
own — *read between the lines of the comments rather than just reading the
comments* — but note that the second video replaces the comment section with an
in-person sample, and Rex should not treat comment volume as customer evidence
without asking who is commenting.

**Metrics vs. gut.** `mr4Pw66_490` grades competence by the density of metrics
someone tracks. `dMZ-n2KSlxE` puts an unmeasured text message from a respected
owner above platform metrics. Both are his. The boundary appears to be sample
quality: when your buyer is rare and identifiable, a handful of real responses
beats a large number counted from the wrong population.

## When it applies

Always, and the order matters more than the completeness. Skip section 1 and
every later answer describes a business that cannot use the advice. The questions
are also not neutral — several are designed to move the owner, not just to
measure (the $5-for-$1 question, the 10x question, the $70,000 question). Rex
should know which he is doing.

## In his words

> "If we double the lead flow of the business, can you handle that?"

> "So, we have numbers, but they're not useful."

> "So sales is not the constraint in your business. Then why are you asking me
> about the closer framework?"

## Rex's read for our three clients

- **industries** — start at section 1 and expect the answer to be *no*. A Denver
  welding shop with one crew is supply constrained by construction, so "double
  the calls tomorrow" is the question that decides whether marketing is the work
  at all. Then the close-rate read: a shop that wins most of what it quotes is
  underpriced, and price is the one lever that needs no marketing function.
- **turf-monster** — every money and attribution question fails today. Views and
  likes are typed in by a human, no post links to an entry, so any ratio we could
  compute is an artifact of the kind `HGZOxBfnF-E` describes. We are not allowed
  to spend more *or* less on the strength of it.
- **operator-brand** — only section 0 and "why should I listen to you" have
  answers. Sales velocity is zero, so there is no funnel to walk backwards; the
  receipts are unusually strong. Answer "what problem are we solving" before
  anything is published.
