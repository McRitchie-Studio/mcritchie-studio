# One master, and shortcuts everywhere else

## Situation

A document or folder must show up for people who work somewhere else: a lender's
folder, a deal room, another company's Workspace. Or a document is about to be
filed, and the business it concerns is not the business that signs it.

## The pull

Copy it. A copy is one call, it needs nobody's permission on the master, and the
other folder looks complete at once. It is also tempting to file by brand or by
whichever account is at hand, because the template and the conversation are about
the brand.

## What happened

Employment agreements for a business being bought were first filed under the brand,
then copied to a shared folder so people could work on them. With an older set, one
agreement now had three copies, and any edit could miss two of them. The employer
was the holding company, not the brand.

Alex McRitchie set the model. Ask which entity signs the document, and keep the
master in that entity's Workspace, in a folder named for what it holds. When he
wanted the same folder in a lender's folder, the answer was a Drive shortcut. A test
across two Workspaces proved one master: an edit made by the owner read back through
the other account. The same test added a shortcut to a folder a person made by hand,
once the account had Editor on it. He then looked at the result and approved the
model.

A shortcut does not carry access. Anyone not on the master folder reaches the
access screen, so grant access by name as well. The steps are in the
[`marketing-kit`](../../agents/alex/sops/marketing-kit.md#doc-masters) SOP.
