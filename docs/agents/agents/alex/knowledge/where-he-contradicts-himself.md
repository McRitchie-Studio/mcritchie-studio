# Where he contradicts himself

**Kind:** synthesis · **Sources:** 6 videos · **Contrarian:** by construction ·
**Fit:** all three

Every other card in this directory is something he believes. This one is the map
of where he believes two things.

**This is not a complaint.** A practitioner who publishes for years and never
changes position is not learning, and the corpus shows him retiring his own
claims out loud. But a CMO who quotes one half of a live disagreement at a client
and gets ambushed by the other half has damaged the advice AND the advisor. So:
know where the ground moves.

Each pair below was verified on **both** sides against the corpus, not taken from
a summary.

---

## 1. Do shorts feed long-form? He says no, having said yes

| Position | Where |
|---|---|
| Shorts build the audience that watches longs — with the arithmetic: at 15 seconds each it takes **480 shorts** to deliver the exposure of two one-hour pieces, and he plans on roughly **one in ten** shorts being a 2-3x outlier | `dMZ-n2KSlxE` |
| *"they haven't seen any evidence supporting that shorts create long viewers"* — he names the thesis he had been operating on and disowns it | `JOfsujnXxCg` |

His replacement model is **consumption preference**: people who watch long-form
watch long-form, people who watch shorts watch shorts, and they will not change
their preference for you. The exception is cross-platform — someone meets you on
TikTok and later watches a long elsewhere.

He keeps making shorts anyway, for a different stated reason: **facial
recognition for retargeting.** The short has no destination; the ad that follows
it does, and by then the viewer recognises the face.

**Which to use:** the later one. He states the earlier position and then the
evidence against it, in that order.

## 2. Are follower counts vanity or credibility?

| Position | Where |
|---|---|
| *"followers make you famous, business makes you rich"* — views, subscribers and likes do not translate into revenue, opt-ins or sales, and can move in opposite directions | `0S5xsICW8qg` |
| He opens a video by stating **21,393,122 followers**, then says out loud that he did it to establish credibility for what follows | `XsWSvz-aewA` |

**Both are true and they are about different audiences.** The count does not
produce revenue from the people who already follow you; it produces *permission*
with people who have never heard of you. It is a credential, not a funnel.

Rex's rule: never set a follower target as a goal, and never pretend the number
is worthless when borrowing someone's attention for the first time.

## 3. Publish now, or go do things for five years first?

| Position | Where |
|---|---|
| Asked how to build a personal brand, he says go do real things for **five years** first — documenting along the way is allowed, as long as you tell the truth | `ZTSI3DDP_4A` |
| An entire video built on a dietitian publishing **one post and one story every day**, now, to 5,800 followers | `0S5xsICW8qg` |

The reconciliation is probably *what you publish*, not *whether*: the five-year
answer is about building a brand on borrowed authority, and the daily-cadence
answer is about a specialist publishing what she already knows. He does not state
the boundary, so treat this as unresolved.

**This one lands on us directly.** The operator brand has been doing the thing
for a long time without publishing — which makes it the good case under both
halves, not the awkward one.

## 4. Narrow to one subject, or be many things?

| Position | Where |
|---|---|
| The wide-content experiment raised views but dropped CPMs, opt-ins and book sales, so: back to business only. He calls the other content a mistake, and reports a podcast that **grew downloads while organic sales went to basically zero for eighteen months** | `0S5xsICW8qg` |
| He was told not to talk about life philosophy, ignored it, and those "life sprinkles" are now one of the top things people stop him in the street about — *"the more diverse the interests you have, the more diverse an audience you attract"* | `SCi464zfAUM` |

The likely reconciliation — breadth of *person*, narrowness of *subject* — is an
inference nobody in the corpus states. Flagged as inference.

## 5. Does raising price cost you volume?

| Position | Where |
|---|---|
| A product costing $10 to fulfil, priced at $20, moved to $30: *"I just doubled the lifetime gross profit of my business. Doubled."* The arithmetic models **zero volume loss** | `Q2VUuUGfpNY` |
| A portfolio company doubled its price and measured a **35% reduction in conversion** — *"you have to have the stomach for more nos"* — and still tripled profit per sale | `oRMG_HpOAN4` |

**This is the most operationally dangerous pair on the card**, because the first
version is the one people quote and it is a model, not a measurement. Both
conclusions agree — raise the price — but a plan built on the frictionless
version will read the first month as a failure and reverse a change that was
working.

He supplies the guardrail himself: **know your conversion rate before the change,
and give the new price a statistically significant number of shots before
judging it.** Two nos is not a sample.

**Rex's rule:** quote the measured version. Model the price change with attrition
in it, and state the conversion drop you are willing to absorb *before* you make
it.

## 6. Say it many ways, or repeat it unchanged?

| Position | Where |
|---|---|
| Reviewing someone's ads: running the same content over and over *"just looks cheap"* and costs brand | `OQf2Ba-Lp_4` |
| Elsewhere: republish your best content unchanged, keep one fixed repeated intro | corpus, multiple |

Probably repetition *within one campaign* versus republishing *across time*.
Also unstated.

---

## How Rex uses this card

1. **Before quoting doctrine at a client, check this card.** If the topic is on
   it, say that his position moved and which way.
2. **Prefer the later position** where a date is knowable, and say you are doing
   so.
3. **Where the corpus is genuinely unresolved (3, 4, 5), the client's own numbers
   decide** — which is the standing rule anyway. An unresolved contradiction is
   an invitation to run the test, not to pick the half you like.
