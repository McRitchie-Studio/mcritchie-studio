**Contrarian:** in parts, marked below · **Fit:** all three

## The claim

Paid is an **auction for attention**, so the winner is not whoever writes the
cleverest ad — it is whoever can afford to bid the most and still make money
`reisEL_D7xc` `i7bLRKwKSms`. That makes paid a two-part problem, and both parts
have to be solved:

1. **The model decides what you may bid.** LTGP-to-CAC is the permission slip.
2. **The creative decides how far you can actually spend it.** When an account
   will not scale past a number, the market is almost never too small — the ads
   are not good enough to reach the next tranche of people `reisEL_D7xc`.

And one thing paid is **not**: a compounding channel. See *What paid does not
do* below, because the corpus is specific about this and it is the claim most
often misquoted.

## The mechanism

### Creative is the spend ceiling

His diagnostic for a hard ceiling is the **five levels of awareness** (from
Eugene Schwartz): unaware → problem aware → solution aware → product aware →
most aware. Each step outward demands **an order of magnitude better ad**. So
when a business cannot get past $2,000 or $10,000 or $100,000 a day, the
market is usually big enough and the ads simply cannot reach further out
`reisEL_D7xc`.

> "most times, the problem isn't that your market isn't big enough, it's that
> your ads aren't good enough" `reisEL_D7xc`

The companion arithmetic is **watermelon seed density**: reaching colder
audiences costs the same per thousand eyeballs but converts fewer of them, so
sales per thousand impressions falls as you go out. He numbers it as **20:1 in
the warm tranche, 5:1 in the middle, 3:1 at scale — at a hundred times the
spend**, which is far more absolute return `reisEL_D7xc`. A falling ROAS at
scale is therefore expected, not failure, *provided the LTGP supports the lower
ratio*.

His largest portfolio company — roughly $100M a year, spending millions a month
— markets to the **completely unaware** `reisEL_D7xc`.

### The ad creation process — his standing weekly session

Thirteen years of the same routine `reisEL_D7xc`:

| Step | What he does |
|---|---|
| Continuous | Keeps every platform ad-enabled on purpose so he sees ads, and screenshots them into one swipe folder |
| Weekly, first thing | Pulls the swipe file **plus his own best ads from one, two and three years back**, rewatching the first 10 and 30 seconds of each |
| Then | Writes about **50 hooks** — **40 recycled** from proven material, **10 new** |
| Then | **3-5 bodies** (education, a belief to break, a list, steps, a story, proof) |
| Then | **1-3 CTAs** |
| Same day | Films it. He calls it cramming for the test so the swipe file is top of mind |

**Contrarian:** most people invert the 40/10 split and lead with new ideas
`reisEL_D7xc`.

### Front to back — 80% of the effort goes into the first five seconds

He cites Ogilvy: once the headline is written, you have spent **80 cents of the
advertising dollar** `reisEL_D7xc` `JDR-R--4HhM`. Given an hour, he spends
**55 minutes on the first five seconds and 5 minutes on everything after**
`reisEL_D7xc`.

The justification is the law of small numbers. Lifting a show rate from 50% to
75% is a real win but only **1.5x**. Lifting click-through from 1% to 4% is
**4x the throughput of the entire funnel** `reisEL_D7xc`. Nothing downstream can
triple; the rest of the funnel produces 20% or 50% lifts, and a 50% lift is a
monster split test `JDR-R--4HhM`.

Two cases he uses as proof:

- **Dean's infomercial.** A Larry King interview promoting a book: **$250,000
  spent, nothing back.** He flew Larry King back, rebuilt the set, and
  re-recorded **only the first 30 seconds**, leaving the hour intact. The re-run
  did **$100 million** `reisEL_D7xc`.
- **Landing page headline swap.** At an eight-figure portfolio company they
  changed only the headline. **Booking rate +62%, opt-in rate unchanged** — the
  headline did not change *who* opted in, it changed the frame of the promise
  `reisEL_D7xc`.

### What makes a creative work

- **The call out.** Whatever you do to get attention, on a scale from
  hyper-specific to not specific at all. Drop a tray of dishes and everyone
  looks; a child yells "mom" and the moms look; someone says your name and only
  you look. Aim as specific as possible to get the *right* people, broad enough
  to get *many* `reisEL_D7xc`. Build the list by cataloguing competitor ads that
  have been running a long time — long run time means the call out works.
- **Proof, not you.** About **80% of the ads they have run over the years are
  about proof** rather than about him — he has not been in the gym company's ads
  at all for two years `fSbqaTlWaYI`. **Contrarian**, against the instinct to
  build founder presence.
- **Rank proof by how hard it is to fake.** Weak end: an anonymous ten-year-old
  text testimonial. Strong end: someone who looks exactly like the prospect, on
  stage, live, in person, naming their own numbers. The axes: in person > virtual,
  live > recorded, looks like me > doesn't, the exact result I want > close to it
  `fSbqaTlWaYI`.
- **Your promise is not unique, your proof is.** Everyone in a market promises
  the same outcome, so differentiate on **mechanism** and on how believable it is
  `fSbqaTlWaYI`.
- **Third grade reading level.** Run copy through a free reading-level tool and
  edit down. Ninth-grade copy disqualifies roughly two-thirds of people who can
  read. Short sentences, small words, big promises, big proof; fewer adverbs and
  better verbs `JDR-R--4HhM`.
- **Every line sells the next line.** The copy sells the click, the landing page
  headline sells the opt-in, the opt-in sells showing up `JDR-R--4HhM`.
- **Video has higher volatility than images.** Your best ads will be video and
  your worst ads will be video. So when statics beat video, the finding is *your
  video is weak*, not *our market prefers images* `N5MExtki_VI`. **Contrarian.**
- **Judge it at phone size.** Text yourself the creative. One reviewed ad lost
  the website line, the phone number and the offer text at phone size — all that
  survived was a photo and four words. Rules that fall out: geography in the copy
  headline, offer in the image large enough to read, **drop the logo entirely for
  direct response** `OQf2Ba-Lp_4`.

### CTA formulas

- **1-3 CTAs per ad**, written in the same weekly session as the hooks and
  bodies `reisEL_D7xc`.
- **Contextual, never copy-pasted.** Match the CTA to the topic of the piece it
  sits in. He calls copy-pasted CTAs a huge mistake, including the habit of
  splicing a pre-recorded ad in a different shirt with different energy
  `pLhQOYMGa88`.
- **Thirty short asks.** List every CTA you would ever make (buy, review, tag,
  share, register, grab the magnet), write **five variants of each** for about
  thirty, run each **five to ten seconds** rather than three minutes, and place
  them at the beginning, middle and end `WrCt0R3FBFs`.
- **Splice a five-second CTA onto an organic winner.** The worked example he
  wrote live, as the corpus renders it (a paraphrase, not a verbatim line): if
  this is cool and you want an 18-point inspection, it is absolutely free, click
  the link and we will come straight to your house `OQf2Ba-Lp_4` `N5MExtki_VI`.
- **The PS is the second most read line in any advertising**, after the headline;
  the PPS is third. In an ad, use it as a disclaimer naming who this is *not*
  for, then the click — or a one-sentence recap of every point of persuasion for
  the person who skipped to the bottom `JDR-R--4HhM`.

### Volume — think in hundreds, and spend 80% of it on winners

> "volume negates luck" `reisEL_D7xc`

The gap between you and the people ahead of you is usually not 2x or 3x volume,
it is often 100x `reisEL_D7xc`.

| Allocation | Rule |
|---|---|
| **70/20/10** | 70% of creative resources on the thing that is working, 20% on adjacent permutations, 10% on wild ideas. He attributes the split to Google and explicitly corrects a questioner who proposed 80/20 `fSbqaTlWaYI` |
| **80% reskinning** | 80% of creative resources go to taking winning ads and reskinning them, not making new ones `fSbqaTlWaYI` |
| **From one winner** | The "Kale" testimonial ad became **about a hundred versions** — black and white, sepia, reordered back end, swapped hooks, different headlines, subheads, fonts, ad copy `fSbqaTlWaYI` |
| **From twenty winners** | Take your **20 highest-converting ads of all time**, record each in **4 different settings** → **80 ads** from proven material alone `j2TZMFkj71Q` |
| **Statics** | Bump the N to **~100 image variants** instead of 12-16, sourced from the camera roll `N5MExtki_VI` |
| **The three-second slice** | Take the top 5-10% of your ad library, study the first three seconds, and **literally paste those three seconds onto another hundred ads** — 10 to 50x more creative per winner `fSbqaTlWaYI` |

Two supporting rules. **Once the copy is right, you stop changing it** — they
spent tens of millions on Skool ads last year and **never changed the copy
once**; the iteration is all creative and hooks around fixed words
`N5MExtki_VI`. And **make every customer make you an ad**: gate a bonus behind
the customer filming themselves using the product in their first seven days, so
onboarding and creative production are the same act — at 200 customers a launch,
that is 200 ads `j2TZMFkj71Q`. Generalised, that is the **ad machine**: one
person advertises → a customer comes in → has a great experience → the
experience is documented → the documentation is advertised → it gets the next
customer. *"I want a fusion reactor"* `N5MExtki_VI`.

**Organic is the free test bed.** Post daily, watch what gets disproportionately
saved and shared, then port the winners into paid and cold email `MD5-HByRxoA`
`N5MExtki_VI` `OQf2Ba-Lp_4`. He says it plainly for the cash-constrained:
*anybody who is broke, consider doing that* `N5MExtki_VI`.

### Landing pages and the tests that matter

- **One split test a week, forever.** Decided in a weekly marketing meeting with a
  fixed agenda: what was last week's test, what won, did we beat control. The
  constraint is the point — it forces you to pick the single highest-expected
  change. 52 a year for five years compounds `9Dc2UQbQNTo`. **Contrarian**, and
  note it is the *opposite* discipline from paid creative, where he wants
  hundreds of variants at once.
- **~90% of their landing page tests are the headline or the image beneath it**,
  because almost all the gain is above the fold. Order: headline first, then the
  image; if a new headline makes the old image nonsensical, test the pair as one
  unit `9Dc2UQbQNTo`.
- **Every funnel step costs about half the traffic** `OQf2Ba-Lp_4`. So ads land
  on a **standalone page whose only function is conversion**, never the home
  page: shrink or kill the logo, nav becomes a hamburger, phone number on the top
  bar, offer headline above the fold, form immediately beneath, then locations,
  then FAQs. Nothing else. Then **redirect every existing site button to it**,
  which lifts conversion on traffic you are already paying for `OQf2Ba-Lp_4`.
- **A lead magnet in front of a direct ask captures 3-5x the opt-ins from the
  same hundred visitors** `Mst4hreQYl0`. Same traffic, so the gain is pure
  conversion and costs nothing in media. A lead magnet is *a complete solution to
  a narrow problem* whose solution reveals the problem your core offer solves
  `Mst4hreQYl0`. Name it with one of four formulas: number + outcome + time
  frame; how to get the good thing without the bad thing; adjective + type + good
  thing; X mistakes that prevent the good thing `Mst4hreQYl0`.
- **Friction is a dial, and it is a three-way diagnosis.** Too many bad leads →
  add friction. Not enough leads → remove friction. **Almost no friction and the
  leads are still bad → the targeting is wrong, not the funnel** `fSbqaTlWaYI`.
  Good friction (a VSL on the page) raises lead quality; bad friction (a slow
  page) raises nothing.
- **VSL structure**, five to seven minutes: hook → proof, promise, plan, ideally
  with a picture → an **open loop on pricing** ("I'll explain pricing") to hold
  them to the end → the buying education → price ranges and delivery expectations
  → call setup `HGZOxBfnF-E`.

### What to run first, and in what order

For anyone with existing traffic, the first two campaigns are harvesting, not
demand creation:

1. **Global retargeting across every platform.** Pixel every page, retarget site
   visitors across the Google Display Network, Instagram, Facebook and YouTube.
   He calls it *the shadow funnel* and the easiest ROAS available, because you
   already paid for that traffic `fSbqaTlWaYI`.
2. **Bid on your own brand terms** and every permutation of them. *"There's no
   PPC campaign for your own terms that doesn't make money. I haven't seen it
   ever happen in my entire life"* `fSbqaTlWaYI`. Leaving the space open lets a
   competitor bid on your name and scoop customers you created; if they do, you
   will always beat them on profitability on your own name `OQf2Ba-Lp_4`.

Then: **fix the funnel before you raise spend.** His stated order on a local
service business was price raise → pay down debt → change the funnel → fix the
ads → *then* increase spend → then retargeting and branded search → then outbound
events. The reason is sequencing risk, not thrift: fixing the lander and the
creative may already double or triple lead flow at current spend, at which point
you discover you do not have enough technicians. Raising spend first buys that
problem at a higher price `OQf2Ba-Lp_4`. **Contrarian.**

### Scale or kill — how he decides

He decides on **ratio and capacity**, almost never on cost per lead.

- **The permission ratio.** At least **3:1 LTGP to CAC** — CAC at most a third of
  lifetime gross profit, because you cannot acquire at break-even and still run
  the business `reisEL_D7xc`. But 3:1 only holds **if lead generation, conversion
  and delivery are all automated**; each function with people in it raises the
  floor by roughly three — **6:1 with two automated, 9:1 with one, 12:1+ with
  none** `i7bLRKwKSms`. A mostly-manual service business needs 12:1 or better.
- **Then get the CAC back inside 30 days**, often by adding a one-time upfront
  charge, so scaling is self-funding rather than capital-hungry `reisEL_D7xc`.
- **Scale hard when the ratio is absurd.** All the money he calls crazy money came
  from **30:1 to 200:1**, and those windows do not last. A huge starting ratio is
  an indicator of room: *$1,000 a day at 100:1 suggests $100,000 a day at 5:1 is
  reachable* `reisEL_D7xc`. Maintenance-era businesses run 5:1, 8:1, 10:1, and a
  10:1 will often fall to 5:1 the moment you scale it.
- **The spend-ceiling question.** *"If for every dollar you gave me, I gave you
  $5 back, what would your budget of dollars that you'd give me be?"* The only
  correct answer is as many dollars as possible, recycled `OQf2Ba-Lp_4`. He used it on businesses running 10:1,
  20:1 and 30:1 on a $1,000-a-month budget. And on a profitable operator capping
  CPA at $50 for no stated reason: *"Why don't you spend up to $100 CPA?"*
  — she got it back in the first month `j2TZMFkj71Q`.
- **The one legitimate reason not to spend more is untrustworthy attribution**,
  which is a data problem to fix, not a reason to stay small `OQf2Ba-Lp_4`.
- **Kill on capacity, not on cost.** Before anything else: *if we doubled lead
  flow tomorrow, could you handle it?* If no, the business is supply constrained
  and marketing is the wrong work `OQf2Ba-Lp_4`. At an assisted living facility
  running 20:1 with eight beds left, his answer was spend more, **dialled against
  the known fill rate**, and turn it off when full `fSbqaTlWaYI`.
- **Do not kill for being expensive — check the ratio.** A $5,000 LTV at a $500
  CAC (10:1) is a better business than a $1,000 LTV at a $200 CAC (5:1), even
  though customers cost more than twice as much `i7bLRKwKSms`. **Contrarian.**
- **Only cost to acquire matters.** Intermediate funnel percentages — opt-in
  rates, show rates — do not. Told that a year-long split test contradicted his
  own prediction, he dropped his prior on the spot: it does not make sense to
  him, so he is not going to mess with it `N5MExtki_VI`. **Contrarian**, and it is
  also a rule about evidence: a year-long split test outranks a best practice,
  including his.
- **A rising CAC is not a failure signal.** *Whatever your cost per customer is
  today is probably the cheapest it will ever be* — CPMs rise, competitors enter,
  and scaling itself pushes you into colder audiences `i7bLRKwKSms`. Plan the
  model against a rising CAC, not today's CAC.
- **Broadening the funnel to lower cost buys trash.** Owners widen targeting, get
  way more and way worse customers, then blame the customers `i7bLRKwKSms`.
  **Contrarian** — and see the disagreement section, because this collides with
  the awareness-ladder advice.
- **An impossible ratio is an attribution problem.** A reported 0.41:1 LTGP to
  CAC would mean losing money on 80% of revenue, which was not happening — so the
  number is false, and there is no basis to spend more *or* less until it is
  fixed. The ballsier honest test he floats is **turning all the ads off and
  watching what happens to sales** `HGZOxBfnF-E`.

### Launch spend cadence

For a time-boxed launch with a four-week runway `j2TZMFkj71Q`:

| Window | Share of budget | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Week 1 | ~5%, 10% max | **Creative testing only** |
| Weeks 2-3 | ~10% and ~15% | Flat while scaling up on the chosen creative |
| Week 4 | **60-70%** | Of which roughly **50% of total spend lands in the last three days** |

He says he would put 75% in the final week if it were operationally possible,
and that at the end of his own launch he was spending **$500,000 a day**
`j2TZMFkj71Q`. The account has to already be warmed up to spend at that rate.

## What paid does not do

**It is a one-time 3-5x, not a compounding one.** Offered to a $2.5M coaching
business that wanted to double, explicitly flagged as typical rather than a
promise, and conditioned on having reached that revenue **on organic alone**:

> "the paid side is going to give you call it like a one time 3 to 5x off of a
> baseline" `BYpTRiRqS1Y`

The word he stresses is **one time**. The mechanism is the audience pyramid: a
hot top layer that buys, sitting on a base of eyeballs. **Organic grows the
base**, so the monetised percentage holds and the dollar figure rises. **Paid
leaves the base the same and moves the monetisation line down into it** —
harvesting, not building `BYpTRiRqS1Y`. Short term, doubling the business is
just pulling the ad lever; on a ten-year horizon you run both, planting with
organic while ads skim.

Two more things paid does not do on its own:

- **It does not survive a warm-only sales motion.** A funnel built on your own
  audience *will* convert worse on cold traffic — *"you will not convert the same
  percentage you currently do. By a lot"* — and the economics of the whole funnel
  change and need re-adjusting `BYpTRiRqS1Y`. The size of the runway is price ×
  how unaware the buyer is `reisEL_D7xc`.
- **It does not compensate for a bad sales motion.** Great ads plus a bad sales
  motion does not work, and great sales plus bad ads does not work. Build them in
  tandem `BYpTRiRqS1Y`.

And a thing paid *does* do that is easy to miss: **whitelisting**, where a
sponsor runs a creator's own organic post as their paid ad. The business pays to
advertise your face, so they make you bigger, and they get a higher conversion
rate because you are better on camera `oRqnTOT9ZG8`.

## Where the corpus disagrees with itself

**Broaden to scale, or narrow to scale?** Both are stated flatly.

| Position | Where |
|---|---|
| Broadening the funnel to lower cost per customer reliably buys **more customers and worse ones**, and then owners blame the customers | `i7bLRKwKSms` |
| To break a spend ceiling you **must** reach colder, less-aware audiences, and accept the ratio falling 20:1 → 5:1 → 3:1 | `reisEL_D7xc` |

The reconciliation that fits both — and which he holds inside a single session
without naming it — is that these are **two different axes**. On **awareness**,
go broader: add pain-aware hooks so the targeting can open, because an account
whose every ad speaks to product-aware buyers triples its CPA the moment you
raise spend `j2TZMFkj71Q`. On **call-out**, go narrower: *"you're draining 100
ponds of audiences to get your scale more than trying to advertise to the
oceans"* `j2TZMFkj71Q`. Broad problem, narrow person. **He never states it as one
rule — flagged as inference.**

**Repetition.** The 80%-reskin doctrine `fSbqaTlWaYI` sits against his review
note that running the same content over and over *"just looks cheap"* and costs
brand `OQf2Ba-Lp_4`. This pair is already on
[`where-he-contradicts-himself.md`](where-he-contradicts-himself.md) § 5. The
resolution the paid material suggests is that **reskinning IS saying it in
different ways** — a hundred versions of one winner is the opposite of running
one file unchanged. Still an inference.

**One test or a hundred?** Landing pages get **exactly one split test a week**
`9Dc2UQbQNTo`; creative gets a hundred variants at once `N5MExtki_VI`
`j2TZMFkj71Q`. He never reconciles these, and they are probably about cost:
creative variants are nearly free to produce, page variants each cost a week of
traffic.

## When it applies

Paid is one of the **Core Four** `reisEL_D7xc`, and it is the one-to-many box
aimed at strangers. It applies when:

- **You can name LTGP and CAC.** If you cannot, you are not ready to bid, and an
  impossible ratio means fix attribution first `HGZOxBfnF-E`.
- **You are demand constrained.** Double the lead flow tomorrow — could you serve
  it? `OQf2Ba-Lp_4`
- **You already have something that works.** Retargeting and branded search
  monetise demand you created elsewhere; they are the first campaigns precisely
  because they are harvesting `fSbqaTlWaYI`.

It does **not** apply as a substitute for a business that does not work. *All
advertising works — it's just a matter of efficiency*; "X doesn't work for us"
rewrites to "I don't know how to make X work for us," which is solvable
`reisEL_D7xc`. And a claim that a channel is saturated is a belief problem: he
asked a $2M agency claiming it had saturated Facebook to *"explain to me why
every sale that is keeping all of them in business is somehow not sales that you
should be making"* — competitors in the category were spending perhaps $4M a
month in the same market `reisEL_D7xc`.
