# SOP — Content Sprint

**Owner:** Rex · **Runs:** weekly, per client, once a constraint has been named
by [`constraint-diagnosis.md`](constraint-diagnosis.md).

A sprint tests **one** thing at volume and reads the result. If you cannot say
what this week's sprint was testing, it was not a sprint, it was posting.

Run it start to finish from this file.

---

## Precondition

A named constraint and a prediction from the diagnosis SOP. **If there is no
constraint, stop and run that SOP instead.** A content sprint against an unnamed
constraint produces work that cannot be wrong, which means it cannot teach.

## Step 1 — Monday: score last week before planning this one

Open last sprint's prediction. Answer in this order, out loud:

1. What did we predict?
2. What happened?
3. Was the prediction high, low, or right?
4. **Does the constraint change?**

Only question 4 decides this week's work. If the constraint did not change and
the prediction was low, the answer is usually more of the same at higher volume —
not a new idea. Changing the test every week is how a year passes with no
conclusion.

## Step 2 — Name this week's ONE test

Write it as a sentence with a number in it:

> "If we publish 30 posts that open on the cost of the problem instead of the
> product, entries will rise from 12 to 20 this week."

One variable. If you are testing the hook AND the offer AND the platform, you
will learn nothing from any result, good or bad.

## Step 3 — Set the volume target, then double the batch

Decide how many reps, by when. Then build **more than you think you need**,
because the failure mode of every content plan is that it runs dry on Wednesday
and the sprint quietly becomes three posts.

Rules of the batch:

- Same offer across the batch. Vary only the tested variable.
- Ugly ships. A rep that exists beats a rep that is still being refined.
- Build the whole batch before publishing any of it. Producing daily turns a
  volume test into a stamina test.

## Step 4 — Voice pass: Mason

Hand the batch to **Mason**. He owns brand voice and he can veto a line.

The seam, so it does not get relitigated weekly: **Rex sets what the batch is
testing and how many; Mason sets whether a given sentence is ours.** If volume is
the constraint, Mason ships at his floor rather than his ceiling — and his floor
is still a floor, not a suggestion. He will not pass a claim the product cannot
back, and Rex backs him on that against his own campaign.

Anything visual goes to **Shannon**. Anything claiming what the product does gets
a check from **Avi** before it leaves the building.

## Step 5 — Instrument BEFORE publishing

Every item in the batch gets a way to tell it apart in the result: a distinct
link, a code, a landing path, something. **An uninstrumented batch is a batch you
will argue about instead of learn from.**

This is currently the ecosystem's weakest link. The existing content records hold
view, like, comment and share fields that are filled in **by a human typing
them**, and nothing reads performance back from the platforms. Until that is
fixed, instrumentation means a manual weekly read at a fixed time — put it in the
sprint, name who does it, or it will not happen.

## Step 6 — Publish on a fixed cadence

Use the lane that already exists rather than inventing one: the contents board
carries a record per item through hook, script, assets, assembly and posted, and
the publish step is a human click on X or TikTok.

Publish at a fixed time. A cadence is a variable too, and a random one
contaminates the test you are actually running.

## Step 7 — Friday: read it and say which

- Did the number move?
- Which items carried it? (This is why step 5 exists.)
- Was the prediction high, low, or right — **say it plainly**.
- Kill, continue, or double.

Record the answer somewhere the next sprint will read it. A sprint whose result
lives only in a chat window did not happen.

---

## The three failure modes this SOP exists to prevent

1. **Posting without a test.** Activity that cannot be wrong teaches nothing.
2. **Changing the variable every week.** Twelve one-week experiments with twelve
   different variables is not twelve experiments.
3. **Running dry on Wednesday.** Volume decided on Monday and abandoned by
   midweek reads, in the data, exactly like a failed idea. It is not — it is an
   unfinished one, and it will get a good idea killed.
